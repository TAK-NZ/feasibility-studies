# Pass criteria, evaluated on the test set (offshore)

Generated by `scripts/s09_metrics.py` from `results/ens_metrics.csv`, `data/runs/det_errors.parquet` and the saved particle clouds. Machine-readable version: `results/pass_criteria.csv`. All numbers are test-set numbers (drifters never used for any tuning) unless a section says otherwise. Intervals are 95% percentile intervals of a cluster bootstrap that resamples whole drifters (2,000 replicates, seed 42); the interval is also computed with 96 h blocks as clusters, and the wider of the two is quoted as "CI".

## Criterion 1: offshore median 24 h error against the hand-calculation baseline

The baseline (B1) is the operator's hand calculation: datum, one drift vector from the same current (and, for lost drifters, 0.5% of the forecast wind) evaluated once at the datum, and an expanding circle; its windage and circle growth rates were tuned on the tuning set (`work/baseline.md`). The model is OpenDrift with the configuration chosen on the tuning set (`work/tuning.md`). Model error is the distance from the observed position to the centroid of the 500-particle ensemble (the product that carries the contours; this was the headline definition, chosen before the deterministic-particle and no-offset variants were computed; the three agree within 0.3 percentage points, and all are shown). "Reduction" is 1 - median(model error) / median(B1 error) on the same windows.

\* n_eff is the approximate number of independent windows: windows / (1 + (m - 1) x rho), where m is the mean number of windows per drifter and rho the intra-drifter correlation of the 24 h error (ANOVA estimate). It does not account for drifters that share weather on the same date; the by-block interval does.

The row labelled "best configuration chosen ON THE TEST SET" picks, among all 108 lost-class (or 5 drogued-class) deterministic configurations, the one with the lowest test-set median. It is selection on test data, so it is an optimistic ceiling for what choosing among the variants could give, and its interval ignores the selection.

**The model does not clearly beat the baseline offshore at 24 h.** The pooled reduction is 23.6% (CI 20.2% to 26.8%), the whole interval is below 30%. For lost drifters it is 26.3% (CI 21.9% to 29.7%) and for drogued drifters 19.3% (CI 13.9% to 24.9%). Criterion 1 is therefore not met in the agreed form. At 48 h the pooled reduction is 31.3% (CI 27.5% to 35.4%), but 24 h is the agreed lead.

### If the model does not clearly beat the baseline: what would need to change

The numbers below are what the data show. The remedies are inferences from them, not things this study tested.

- **Size of the gap.** A 30% reduction would need a pooled 24 h median of 9.58 km. The model gives 10.45 km, so it is 0.87 km short (the median error would have to fall by a further 8%).
- **Forcing choices do not close it.** Across the 18 lost-class variants (currents with and without tide, 6-hourly and hourly, GFS, ECMWF or the mean of both, Stokes drift on or off) with windage tuned on the tuning set, the test-set 24 h median ranges from 10.51 to 11.30 km; the five drogued-class current variants range from 9.80 to 10.26 km. Even the best configuration chosen on the test set reaches only 27.2% (lost) and 22.5% (drogued). Adding tides, Stokes drift or a second wind source is therefore not where the missing skill is: of the 21 paired differences against the chosen variant, 0 have an interval that excludes zero (`results/metrics_test_variants.csv`). One note on the drogued class: the 15 m current variants (the drogue depth) were best at 24 h on both sets (22.5% and 22.2% on the test set), but within the 2% tie band on the tuning set, and the pre-set tie rule (lower 48 h median) picked the no-tide surface current, which gives 19.3%. The rule was not changed after the test set was seen.
- **Part of the error is a drift that persists and the analysis misses.** B3, which carries the drifter's own velocity from the 12 h before the window forward, has a pooled 24 h median of 7.52 km (45% below B1 and 28% below the model). A real operator does not have that information, but it is consistent with a good part (not all) of the 24 h error being a drift that persists for a day or more and is absent from the 1/12 degree analysis. The forcing check points the same way: for the chosen currents the vector RMSE against the drifters' own velocity is 0.22 m/s (lost) and 0.19 m/s (drogued), against observed mean speeds of 0.22 and 0.19 m/s, with vector correlation 0.62 and 0.60 (`results/forcing_check.csv`, test set).
- **What the evidence points to.** (i) Better current forcing is the lever: higher-resolution or regional ocean forcing (the brief's example), or a different or additional ocean model for eddy and boundary-current fields. (ii) Anything that anchors the cloud to an observed drift, such as a datum marker buoy or a drifter report, would address the persistence-type error that B3 exposes. (iii) A hindcast with archived forecasts instead of analysis currents would show how much of this is lost live; it can only get worse than the numbers here. None of these was tested in this study, and (i) in particular is untested: the data show the 1/12 degree analysis is not enough for the 30% target at 24 h, not that a finer product would be.
- **The baseline is not equally strong for both classes.** For drogued drifters B1 is no better than leaving the datum where it is (B0 median 12.86 km vs B1 12.64 km at 24 h), so the 30% line is against a weak reference there and the model still falls short (19.3%). Against B0 (no calculation at all) the pooled reduction is 28.2% (CI 24.8% to 31.6%).

## Criterion 2: calibration of the contours

The 95% and 50% contours are highest-density regions of the 500-particle cloud (Gaussian kernel density estimate, 3 km minimum bandwidth). The cloud is the OpenDrift ensemble with the Level-1 uncertainty setting chosen on the tuning set plus a persistent velocity offset applied to each particle outside OpenDrift (0.125 m/s for lost, 0.1 m/s for drogued, per component), selected on the tuning set so that the 24 h 95% hit rate was at least 0.90 with the smallest area. That offset is an extra tuned parameter, not an OpenDrift feature. Without it the chosen native keys hold almost nothing (last three rows of the table).

At 24 h the model's 95% contour has a median area of 2,067 km2 against 3,575 km2 for the baseline circle (42% smaller) at hit rates of 92% and 94%. For lost drifters: 2,216 vs 3,575 km2 at 94% vs 95%. The two are not matched on hit rate exactly, and the circle's growth rate was fitted to a single 24 h target, so this is an indication of a smaller search area at similar coverage, not a precise equal-coverage comparison.

Reliability at 24 h (all offshore test windows; nominal contour level against the fraction of observed endpoints inside; `results/reliability_offshore_test.csv`, `figures/fig2_calibration.png`): 10% holds 12.5%, 50% holds 56.5%, 80% holds 80.5%, 90% holds 88.2%, 95% holds 92.0%, 99% holds 96.7%. Up to about the 80% level the contours hold slightly more than nominal; at 90% and above they hold less. The cloud is somewhat too concentrated in the middle and its tails are too light, so the 95% contour is reliable only because the offset was tuned for it, and the 99% contour would not be.

Short leads are under-dispersed: the pooled 95% contour holds 80.2% at 6 h and 88.2% at 12 h, and for drogued drifters 74.7% at 6 h. One persistent offset grows linearly from zero, while real short-lead errors do not. The 80% pass line is reached at every lead for the pooled sample (6 h only just), for the lost class at every lead, and for the drogued class from 12 h. The nominal 95% is not reached at any lead for the pooled sample (best: 94.3% at 48 h); only the lost class reaches it, at 48 h (95.9%).

## Reading these results

- The test uses best-estimate analysis currents, not archived forecasts, so live skill would be lower by an amount this test cannot measure.
- Drifters are 35 cm floats (drogued ones follow the 15 m current), not people or boats; the tuned windage of 0.5% does not transfer to a SAR object.
- Every window starts from an exactly known position and time. A real datum has neither.
- The tuned offset layer means the calibration shown is for settings fitted to this region and period; it was fitted on 40% of drifters and tested on the other 60%.
- Strata results (season, wind class, ECMWF grid period) are shown without multiple-comparison correction; differences between strata are mostly within their intervals.
- Windows without a pre-window drifter velocity (13 windows of 4,086) use B0 in the B3 reference line only.
